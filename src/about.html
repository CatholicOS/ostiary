About Ostiary

Until 1972 the Latin Church ordained men to the minor order of ostiarius: the porter, the doorkeeper. He held the keys, opened and shut the doors, and rang the bell. The order was suppressed. The work was not. Ushers are what is left of it, and this app is named for them.

What it does

Two things a parish currently does in a group text and on a paper sign-up sheet taped inside the sacristy door.

Formation. Eight modules a new usher works through, with progress recorded per person, so a coordinator can tell who has actually been trained before handing them a collection basket.

Organization. Mass-by-Mass rosters ushers sign up for themselves, sub requests when somebody cannot make a slot, and team meetings with agendas, minutes, and attendance.

What it deliberately does not know

Four modules are intentionally incomplete.

The collection, communion, emergencies, and safe environment are governed by policy that is diocesan and parish specific. Communion is on that list because low-gluten host and separate-chalice provisions vary parish to parish, and getting it wrong means somebody either does not receive or gets sick. This app does not cite that policy, because no such policy document was read while the curriculum was written, and a confident invented citation is the most dangerous thing formation software can produce. An usher would believe it.

Those four modules carry a standing banner and a slot the coordinator fills in from the parish's own written policy. If that slot is empty at your parish, the emptiness is the finding, not a bug.

Seeded Mass times are placeholders

The demo parish ships with a plausible Sunday pattern that was never checked against a real parish schedule. Every seeded slot is flagged ! Time not confirmed until a coordinator confirms it. A roster built on guessed Mass times is worse than an empty one, because people trust it.

How secure this is, precisely

Ushers sign in with a shared parish code and pick their name off a roster. There is no per-person password. Coordinators additionally enter a passphrase, stored as a PBKDF2-SHA256 hash, which is what unlocks any write to the roster or the schedule.

That is shoulder-surf resistant, not attacker resistant. Anyone holding the parish code can read that parish's roster and sign themselves up for a Mass. For a volunteer usher list that is an acceptable trade. It would not be acceptable for anything sacramental, financial, or safe-environment related, and the app should not be extended to hold those without a real identity provider first.

The database holds names, emails, and phone numbers, which is why it is kept separate from the public parish directory that lachurches.org serves.

Accessibility

Module 3 teaches ushers to read an access need before somebody has to ask. Whether the building can actually meet that need is a different question, and a different app.
